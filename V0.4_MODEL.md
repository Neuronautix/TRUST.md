# TRUST.md v0.4 proposed normative model

**Status:** accepted design for v0.4 implementation · **Date:** 2026-07-21

This document fixes the semantic contract that the v0.4 schema, validator,
examples, and user documentation will implement. Until those artifacts are
merged and a v0.4 release is published, v0.3 remains the current released
contract.

The key words **MUST**, **MUST NOT**, **SHOULD**, and **MAY** are normative.

## 1. Evidence records and assessments are different objects

A subject identifies an evidence record or other assessable object. It remains
authoritative for its observations, producer provenance, factual quality
control, version, and lifecycle. An assessment is an attributable,
protocol-dependent interpretation of inspectable information about a subject.

An assessment MUST link to a subject and MUST NOT copy, replace, or mutate the
subject. Trust is not an intrinsic or universal property of the subject.
Schema conformance does not make an assessment canonical or certify either the
assessment or its subject.

## 2. Required subject registry

Every v0.4 declaration MUST contain a `subjects` array with at least one
subject. The registry is independent of `assessments`, so a declaration can
represent evidence for which no assessment exists. `assessments` MAY be empty
or absent; absence means that the declaration publishes no assessment.

Each subject MUST declare:

- a manifest-local `id` used by assessment references;
- a `type` such as `repository`, `artifact`, `claim`, `claim-evidence`, or
  `evidence-record`;
- an authoritative `identifier`; and
- either an explicit `version` or an immutable `snapshot` with a digest.

The subject identity tuple is the authoritative identifier plus its version,
or the immutable snapshot identifier plus digest. A mutable locator without
one of those identity constraints is invalid. Assessments MUST resolve their
`subject` reference to exactly one registry entry.

## 3. Plural, independently versioned assessments

`assessments` is a collection. Zero, one, or many assessments MAY refer to the
same subject. Each assessment MUST have:

- `series_id`, stable across versions of the same assessment lineage;
- `id`, an immutable identifier for this specific assessment version;
- `version`, the assessment's explicit version;
- `subject`, a reference to one entry in `subjects`;
- `assessed_by`, separating human and automated assessors;
- `protocol.identifier` and `protocol.version`;
- `assessed_at`, an ISO 8601 datetime with an explicit offset;
- `review_status`, as provenance rather than a quality dimension;
- `independence`, as a declared state;
- `status`, the lifecycle state; and
- `limitations`, including unresolved disagreements when known.

Assessment `id` values MUST be unique within a declaration. The tuple of
`series_id` and `version` MUST also be unique. The validator MUST NOT select an
assessment as canonical because it appears first, is stored with the subject,
or has a later date.

The v0.4 front matter retains the v0.3 common descriptive, producer,
governance, and epistemic-support metadata unless this document explicitly
changes it. The singular v0.3 `assessment` field is not a v0.4 assessment and
MUST NOT be silently reinterpreted as one.

The following shape is illustrative of the accepted field names and
cardinalities; PR 2 will encode it without changing these semantics:

```yaml
subjects:
  - id: "subject-001"
    type: "evidence-record"
    identifier: "https://doi.org/10.example/data"
    version: "1.0"
assessments:
  - series_id: "https://example.org/assessments/series-001"
    id: "https://example.org/assessments/series-001/versions/1"
    version: "1"
    subject: "subject-001"
    purpose: "Inclusion in a declared synthesis"
    fitness_for_purpose: "conditionally-suitable"
    dimensions:
      evidence_support: "direct"
      calibration: "matched"
      source_integrity: "verified"
    assessed_by:
      humans: []
      agents: []
    protocol:
      identifier: "https://example.org/protocols/example"
      version: "1.0"
    evidence_basis:
      - relation: "uses-qc-report"
        identifier: "https://example.org/qc/report-001"
    assessed_at: "2026-07-21T10:00:00Z"
    review_status: "unreviewed"
    independence: "not-declared"
    status: "active"
    limitations: []
```

## 4. Dimensions, purpose, and fitness for purpose

Core assessment dimensions remain decomposable and non-compensatory. v0.4
retains `evidence_support`, `calibration`, and `source_integrity`, including the
distinct `not-assessed` and `not-applicable` states. `review_status` is removed
from `dimensions` and retained only in assessment provenance. Dimensions and
any optional non-probabilistic numeric refinement are scoped to one assessment,
not published as a shared top-level assessment of every subject. An absent
dimension, an explicit missing state, and the lowest assessed value remain
different statements.

`purpose` is optional for a descriptive assessment. A fitness-for-purpose
conclusion MUST declare `purpose` and MUST use one of:

- `suitable`;
- `conditionally-suitable`; or
- `not-suitable`.

`not-assessed` and `not-applicable` remain available where a fitness conclusion
was deliberately not made or does not apply. Conditions and limitations MUST
remain inspectable; a conclusion for one purpose MUST NOT be generalized to
another purpose.

No dimension or fitness conclusion is a probability. Dimensions MUST NOT be
summed, averaged, or treated as compensatory scores.

## 5. Protocol and assessment basis

Protocols are external, domain-specific, versioned objects. Core TRUST.md
describes their identity and assessment structure; it does not impose a single
methodological-quality rubric across domains.

An `evidence_basis` entry links an assessment to an inspectable record and MUST
use one of the core relations:

- `informed-by`;
- `uses-qc-report`;
- `checked-against`;
- `derived-from`.

Extensions MAY define additional relations without changing these meanings.
Every basis entry MUST identify an inspectable record; prose alone is not a
basis link. An `active` assessment whose `review_status` is `agent-reviewed`,
`human-reviewed`, or `adjudicated` MUST link at least one basis record.

The evidence basis may include technical QC, calibration, missingness,
exclusions, artefacts, deviations, uncertainty, source checks, corrections,
independent appraisal, or replication. A link records what informed the
assessment; it does not establish that the linked record is true or sufficient.

## 6. Provenance, review, and independence

`review_status` describes the highest declared review process completed:
`unreviewed`, `agent-reviewed`, `human-reviewed`, or `adjudicated`.
Agent agreement is not human review. Human-reviewed and adjudicated states
require identifiable human provenance; adjudication also requires an
inspectable disagreement and resolution record.

Independence MUST use an explicitly declared state, not a boolean:

- `not-declared`;
- `declared-not-independent`;
- `declared-partially-independent`;
- `declared-independent`.

These are claims made by the publisher. A conforming validator checks identity
and structural conditions only; it does not verify independence.

## 7. Coexistence, disagreement, and aggregation

Assessments from different communities, purposes, protocols, or assessors MAY
coexist even when their conclusions conflict. Coexistence does not require
automatic conflict detection or adjudication. Known disagreements SHOULD be
recorded in limitations or linked records.

A v0.4 declaration MUST NOT publish a top-level aggregate across assessments.
The validator MUST NOT average, rank, reconcile, or otherwise collapse them.
An assessment MAY include a summary whose scope is explicitly limited to that
single assessment and its declared protocol. Source-native and external
assessments MUST remain separate.

## 8. Append-only lifecycle

Assessment versions are append-only. A released assessment MUST NOT be edited
in place. A correction creates a new immutable `id` and `version` in the same
`series_id`, with a backward `supersedes` link. The prior version remains
discoverable; a forward link is derivable and MUST NOT require mutation of the
prior record.

`status` records the lifecycle assertion made by that immutable version. A new
active correction with a `supersedes` link makes the referenced version
effectively superseded without rewriting it. A publisher MAY instead issue a
new status-only lifecycle version when withdrawing, retracting, or explicitly
recording supersession. Consumers determine the current series state from the
append-only chain; a serialized status on an older version is historical, not
mutable current-state metadata.

Lifecycle `status` is one of:

- `active` — the assessment is the publisher's current statement;
- `superseded` — a later assessment version replaces it;
- `withdrawn` — removed from current use without necessarily asserting that
  its conclusion was invalid;
- `retracted` — explicitly repudiated by its publisher.

Withdrawal or retraction is published as a new lifecycle version and requires
a reason. Neither state changes the lifecycle of the subject. Supersession MUST
remain within one series and MUST NOT form a cycle.

## 9. Time and precision

v0.4 replaces the v0.3 assessment `date` with `assessed_at`, an ISO 8601
datetime carrying `Z` or an explicit numeric offset. Native v0.4 records SHOULD
use the actual available precision. A migrated day-precision date uses
`00:00:00Z` as a serialization anchor and MUST declare
`assessed_at_precision: date`; this marker preserves the fact that no event
time or original timezone was known.

## 10. Impact is not quality

Citation, download, and reuse counts MAY appear as scoped impact metadata or
extensions. They MUST NOT populate, modify, weight, or justify assessment
dimensions or fitness conclusions. Popularity, prestige, and cumulative
advantage are not evidence-support or methodological-quality signals.

## 11. Validation boundary

Conformance validates document structure, required provenance, identifiers,
and declared relationships. It does **not**:

- establish the truth of evidence or assessment statements;
- certify methodological quality or assessor competence;
- prove assessor independence;
- establish reproducibility or validate scientific conclusions;
- equate agent agreement with human review; or
- turn review status, impact, or reuse into a quality score.

TRUST.md v0.4 is a proposed convention for publishing contextual,
attributable assessments. It is not an authority that certifies evidence.

## 12. Compatibility

The v0.1, v0.2, and v0.3 schemas remain frozen. Exact version dispatch MUST
validate each declaration only under its declared schema. v0.4 MUST NOT
reinterpret the v0.3 singular `assessment` form or alter historical
diagnostics. Migration is explicit and user-directed; validators MUST NOT
rewrite declarations automatically.

## 13. Non-goals

v0.4 does not define a universal trust score, a universal domain rubric,
automatic conflict resolution, laboratory or author rankings, scientific
certification, or automatic migration. It does not infer quality from
prestige, p-values, replication alone, citations, reuse, or popularity. It
does not import Computational Review TRUST scores, ORAtlas protocol scores, or
the semantics of any article into TRUST.md.

## 14. Bounded implementation questions

The semantic decisions above are closed. PR 2 still needs to document bounded
mechanical choices: the permitted digest algorithms and identifier syntax,
which cross-document supersession checks are possible without dereferencing
the network, and the diagnostic level for relationships that are structurally
valid but cannot be inspected locally. Those choices MUST NOT weaken immutable
identity, basis inspection, append-only lifecycle, or the validation boundary.
